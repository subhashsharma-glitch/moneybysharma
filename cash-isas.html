Cash ISAs, explained

What a cash ISA is, how much you can put in, how transfers work and how your money is protected. Every figure links to its official source. Information, not financial advice.

What is a cash ISA?

A cash ISA is a savings account where the interest isn't taxed. It doesn't use up your Personal Savings Allowance, so it matters most if your interest outside an ISA would go over that allowance. Cash ISAs come in easy access, notice and fixed-rate versions, just like ordinary savings accounts. See the main types of savings account.

What Budget 2026 changed

A cut to the cash ISA limit for under-65s from April 2027 had been planned. At Budget 2026 on 28 October 2026 the government said it will keep the cash ISA limit at £20,000 for all savers over 18 from April 2027. The change is due to be made by regulations in November 2026, so check the latest position. Budget 2026 also announced higher tax rates on savings interest outside ISAs from 6 April 2027 (not law yet). See Budget 2026 for savers.

Moving a cash ISA

To switch providers, contact the provider you want to move to and fill in their ISA transfer form. If you take the money out yourself instead, you can't reinvest that part of your allowance again. Some providers have restrictions or charge for transfers, so check first. Source: GOV.UK – Transferring your ISA (opens in new tab) (Checked 29 October 2026).

Is my money protected?

Cash ISAs with UK-authorised banks and building societies count towards the FSCS limit of £120,000 per person, per authorised firm. Brands that share one banking licence count as one firm. See FSCS protection.
