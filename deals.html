Deals and savings on everyday shopping

Loyalty prices, memberships and subscription prices, taken from the retailers' own pages, with the date each was checked. Information only – not advice.

Information only – not advice. This page points to offers that retailers publish on their own websites. Offers, prices and terms change often, and some are time-limited, personalised or only for new customers. The retailer's own page is always the source of truth: check it before you buy or sign up. Every entry shows the date we last checked it. Where we couldn't read an official page, we show a link only. MoneyBySharma earns nothing from any link on this page, and we don't list promo codes.

Jump to: Supermarkets · Cinema · Subscriptions · High street · Student discount services · How to spot a genuine deal

How to spot a genuine deal

  • Compare unit prices, not shelf prices. GOV.UK explains that unit prices (per kilo, litre or item) let you compare different pack sizes, and that the biggest pack is not always the cheapest per unit. You can usually find the unit price beneath the shelf price or beside the item online.
  • Test the “was” price. A discount is only as real as the price it is measured against. Look at what the item cost on earlier visits or in a price-history record, and compare the final price with other shops. GOV.UK said on 9 August 2026 that a consultation will launch this autumn on whether fake “was” prices, invented discounts and misleading recommended retail prices should be banned, because they are hard to tackle under current laws.
  • Know the loyalty-price rules. Loyalty prices usually apply only if you scan or swipe each time you shop, or link your card to your online account. Sainsbury's terms say Nectar Prices can't be applied after the event, and “Your Nectar Prices” and Tesco's “Your Clubcard Prices” have to be unlocked each week before you shop. Check the shelf label for both the regular price and the member price.
  • Watch for pressure tactics. The CMA has published “red lines” on misleading price-reduction and urgency claims online, such as countdown timers and “selling fast” messages that may not be true.
  • Read the small print on free trials. Check what you pay once the trial or introductory price ends, the minimum term and whether it renews automatically (see subscriptions below).

Supermarkets

Loyalty prices, member offers and price-match schemes, as the supermarkets describe them. Most need you to scan a card or app each time you shop.

Loyalty prices

Tesco Clubcard Prices

Tesco says its “Your Clubcard Prices” are personalised offers for eligible customers, based on what they have bought before.

  • New offers arrive in the Tesco app every Wednesday and last for 7 days, to 11:59pm on the following Tuesday.
  • You have to unlock them in the app or online each week, then scan your Clubcard at the till or link it to your online account.
  • Tesco's own page says Clubcard Prices may not show in your online basket, but you still pay the Clubcard Price and see it on your receipt.

Loyalty prices

Sainsbury's Nectar Prices

Nectar Prices are lower prices on a selected range of products at Sainsbury's, in store (not Sainsbury's Local) and online, for Nectar members.

  • In store, scan or swipe your Nectar card or app at the checkout. Online, add your Nectar card to your Sainsbury's account first.
  • “Your Nectar Prices” are up to 10 personalised offers a week. New ones land each Friday and you have to unlock them before you shop.
  • Sainsbury's terms say Nectar Prices cannot be applied after the event: if you don't scan or unlock first, you don't get the discount.

Aldi: Price Check and price guarantees

Aldi says it compares its prices with competitors' equivalent products every week. Its Price Check page lists dated “Weekly Price Check” comparisons; when we checked on 26 October 2026, the latest one listed was dated 24 August 2026.

Price match

“Aldi Price Match” at Tesco and Sainsbury's

“Aldi Price Match” is run by Tesco and by Sainsbury's, not by Aldi. Each matches the price of selected products against comparable Aldi products.

  • Tesco says it matches selected Tesco products against comparable Aldi products, checks Aldi prices in GB stores and/or online twice a week, and doesn't match Aldi promotional prices. The products vary by week, and it isn't in Northern Ireland stores. Tesco now says a selected range of everyday products is price matched in Tesco Express too, not the full range.
  • Sainsbury's says it covers selected products only, not in Northern Ireland, with prices checked twice weekly. Its terms link to a table of the matched products, which it says is updated regularly.
  • Tesco – Aldi Price Match (opens in new tab)
  • Sainsbury's – Aldi Price Match terms (opens in new tab)
