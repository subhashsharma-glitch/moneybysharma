How to buy gilts through an ISA, a SIPP or a general account

Ways to buy a gilt, and how the tax treatment differs between a stocks and shares ISA, a SIPP and an account with no tax wrapper. Information, not financial advice.

How individuals buy gilts

The DMO says gilts can be bought in the secondary market through a stockbroker or a bank, or through its Purchase and Sale Service, which is open only to its Approved Group of investors. It also says gilts can no longer be bought from the Bank of England, National Savings & Investments or the Post Office (UK Debt Management Office – FAQs: how do I buy gilts? (opens in new tab), checked 26 November 2026). In practice most people use a stockbroker, bank or investment platform. Not every provider offers gilts, so check before you open an account. We do not recommend any provider.

  1. Choose the account type (see the table below).
  2. Pick the gilt. Coupon, maturity date and the list of gilts in issue are on the DMO’s gilts in issue page (opens in new tab). Our calculator shows the yield after tax for a price you enter.
  3. Place the order for an amount of nominal value. The price you see is usually the clean price.
  4. Expect accrued interest. A buyer normally pays accrued interest on top of the clean price. A gilt goes ex-dividend 7 business days before each coupon date, and a buyer who settles after that date does not get that coupon, so the price is adjusted (DMO – About gilts (opens in new tab); HMRC Savings and Investment Manual SAIM4020 – cum div and ex div (gilts: 7 business days) (opens in new tab)).
  5. Check the costs. Brokers and platforms can charge dealing commission and account fees, so check the charges before you deal.

Does the wrapper matter for low-coupon gilts?

Outside a wrapper, a low-coupon gilt’s tax edge comes from the gain being exempt while the coupon is taxed (see the tax article). Inside an ISA or a SIPP neither coupon nor gain is taxed, so the split between coupon and gain stops mattering for tax, and the price, yield and your own needs are what is left to compare. A SIPP also adds tax relief on contributions, but money is locked away until pension age (GOV.UK – Tax relief on pension contributions (opens in new tab)).
