Bank accounts

Types of current account, how switching works, how your money is protected, overdraft rules, and an A–Z of banks. A neutral list – we don't rate or recommend anyone.

Information only – not advice. MoneyBySharma has no affiliation with, and receives no payment or commission from, any bank, building society or app. Nothing here is a recommendation. Accounts, fees and offers change often, so always check the current terms with the provider before you apply.

Types of account

  • Current account. Your everyday account for wages, bills, card payments and transfers. Many have no monthly fee.
  • Packaged account. You pay a monthly fee for extras such as travel, phone or breakdown insurance. Work out whether you'd use, and are eligible for, each benefit. Compare the yearly fee with what the cover would cost you separately, and check you aren't already covered elsewhere.
  • Joint account. Shared by two (or sometimes more) people. Each holder can usually withdraw all the money. Each is also responsible for the full overdraft. A joint account creates a financial association on your credit files, so a lender may look at the other person's record too. When the account closes, the association stays until you ask the credit reference agencies to remove it.
  • Student and graduate accounts. These are for people in higher education. They usually come with an interest-free arranged overdraft, which shrinks after you graduate. Check when the free overdraft ends and what it costs after that.
  • Basic bank account. This has no overdraft and no fees for standard operations, including a failed payment. It lets you receive money, pay bills by Direct Debit and use a debit card. Under the Payment Accounts Regulations 2015, nine designated providers must offer one to people who are legally resident in the UK and either don't have a UK bank account or aren't eligible for the provider's other accounts. The nine are Barclays UK, The Co-operative Bank, HSBC UK, Lloyds Banking Group (including Halifax and Bank of Scotland), Nationwide Building Society, NatWest Group (including RBS and Ulster Bank), Santander UK, TSB and Virgin Money UK (now a brand of Nationwide). For contracts entered into on or after 28 April 2026, a provider must give 90 days' notice and a specific reason before closing a basic account.
  • Business account. If you run a business, its money should usually be kept separate. Business accounts often charge fees for transactions. For FSCS purposes, a limited company has its own £120,000 limit, but a sole trader's personal and business money is added together.

Sources (checked 12 October 2026): FCA – Basic bank accounts: the problems we found and how industry is committing to change; GOV.UK/HM Treasury – Basic bank accounts: July 2023 to June 2024 (5 Nov 2025); FSCS – Banks, building societies and credit unions (limit, shared licences, temporary high balances).

Switching with the Current Account Switch Service (CASS)

  • 7 working days. You pick a switch date with your new bank (not a weekend or bank holiday) and allow 7 working days. Your Direct Debits, standing orders and incoming payments move across automatically.
  • Your old account closes. A full switch closes your old account. Until the switch date you can still use it, but don't set up new payments on it in the 7 working days before.
  • Redirection. Payments still sent to your old account are redirected to the new one, and the sender is told your new details automatically. Pay.UK says redirection lasts at least three years, or longer if it's still needed.
  • The Current Account Switch Guarantee. If something goes wrong in the switch, any interest or charges it causes on either account are refunded.
  • Overdrafts. An overdraft doesn't move automatically. Agree any overdraft with the new bank before you start the switch.

Sources (checked 12 October 2026): Current Account Switch Service – The switching process and common questions; Current Account Switch Service – Common questions (personal); Pay.UK – Current Account Switch Service (redirection period).

FSCS deposit protection: £120,000

On 1 December 2025 the FSCS deposit limit rose from £85,000 to £120,000 per eligible person, per bank, building society or credit union. If a UK-authorised firm fails, the FSCS aims to pay you within seven working days, and you don't need to claim.

  • The limit is per banking licence, not per brand. Some brands share one licence and count as one bank. For example, Halifax is a division of Bank of Scotland plc, and first direct is a division of HSBC UK Bank plc. You can check which brands share a licence on the Financial Services Register or with the FSCS checker.
  • Joint accounts: each holder is covered up to £120,000.
  • Temporary high balances: money from certain life events, such as a house sale, an inheritance or an insurance payout, is protected up to £1.4 million for six months from when it was first deposited.
  • App providers: check the provider is a UK-authorised bank. Money held with an e-money firm isn't covered by the FSCS in the same way.

See also our savings guide.

Sources (checked 12 October 2026): FSCS – Banks, building societies and credit unions (limit, shared licences, temporary high balances).

Overdrafts and fees

  • Arranged overdraft: a borrowing limit agreed with your bank in advance.
  • Unarranged overdraft: when you go overdrawn without an agreed limit, or beyond it. Banks can refuse the payment instead.
  • The 2020 FCA rules. Since 6 April 2020, banks have had to charge for overdrafts using a single, simple annual interest rate. There are no daily or monthly overdraft fees, and no fees for arranging an overdraft of up to £10,000. The unarranged rate can't be higher than the arranged rate. Overdrafts are advertised with an APR, so you can compare them.
  • Buffers: some accounts give an interest-free amount, such as the first few hundred pounds of an arranged overdraft. Interest is charged only on borrowing above it.
  • Failed (refused) payment fees: these are still allowed, but the FCA says they should reasonably reflect the bank's cost of refusing the payment. Basic bank accounts can't charge them.

Sources (checked 12 October 2026): FCA – PS19/16: High-Cost Credit Review, overdrafts policy statement (rules in force 6 April 2020); FCA Handbook – CONC 5C Overdraft pricing.

Deals and switching bonuses

Some banks pay a cash bonus when you move your account using CASS. The usual conditions are:

  • you must use a full switch (closing your old account)
  • you must move a minimum number of Direct Debits
  • you must pay in a set amount, sometimes a salary
  • you may need to make some card payments within a deadline
  • you usually mustn't have held an account with that bank, or its sister brands, before.

Bonuses are paid only once the conditions are met, they can be withdrawn at any time, and they change often. Opening an account usually involves a credit check. A bonus is only one factor, alongside fees, overdraft costs and service.
