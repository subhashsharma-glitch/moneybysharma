How it works: bought today and held to maturity. You pay the clean price plus accrued interest (dirty price). The gross redemption yield (GRY) uses semi-annual compounding (the market convention). The net yield taxes each coupon at your chosen rate, leaves the gain to £100 untaxed (CGT-exempt), and is shown as an annual effective rate. The equivalent savings rate is the AER a savings account would need to pay to match it after tax: net ÷ (1 − tax rate). Simplifications: coupon dates are counted back from maturity, ex-dividend periods and dealing costs are ignored, and the Accrued Income Scheme (which can apply if your gilts' total nominal value is over £5,000) isn't modelled. The 2027/28 options use the savings rates announced at Budget 2026 (24% / 44% / 49%), which still have to pass in Finance Bill 2026-27. If you pay 0% on all coupons, the "equivalent" is simply the gross figure.
