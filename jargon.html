Jargon buster

152 money terms in plain English. Start typing to search.

ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVYZ
Accrued interest
Interest built up on a bond since its last coupon date. The buyer pays it to the seller on top of the clean price.
Active fund
A fund whose manager picks investments aiming to beat a benchmark. Usually charges more than an index fund.
Additional-rate taxpayer
Someone with taxable income over £125,140 (2026/27, rest of UK).
AER
Annual Equivalent Rate – the interest you'd earn over a year if interest were compounded, so you can compare accounts that pay interest at different intervals.
Amortisation
Paying off a loan gradually through regular payments that cover interest and part of the capital.
Annual allowance
The most that can be paid into your pensions each tax year with tax relief – £60,000 for most people in 2026/27.
Annuity
A product that turns a pension pot into a guaranteed income, usually for life.
APR
Annual percentage rate: a yearly rate of the cost of borrowing, including interest and certain compulsory charges, so you can compare credit. See also representative APR.
Arranged overdraft
A borrowing limit on a current account agreed with your bank in advance. Since April 2020 it's charged as a single annual interest rate.
Arrangement fee
A fee some lenders charge to set up a mortgage deal.
Authorised push payment (APP) scam
When you're tricked into sending a bank transfer to a fraudster. Since 7 October 2024, banks must usually reimburse eligible UK Faster Payments and CHAPS victims up to £85,000 per claim.
Auto-enrolment
The legal duty on employers to put eligible workers (aged 22 to State Pension age, earning over £10,000 a year) into a workplace pension.
Avios
Reward points used by British Airways and some other airlines, collected on some credit cards.
Balance transfer
Moving debt from one credit card to another, often at 0% for a period in exchange for a one-off fee.
Bank Rate
The Bank of England's main interest rate, set by its Monetary Policy Committee. It influences savings and mortgage rates.
Banking licence
A bank's authorisation. The FSCS limit applies per licence, so brands sharing one licence count as one bank.
Basic bank account
A fee-free account with no overdraft. Nine designated UK banks must offer one to eligible people who can't get a standard account.
Basic-rate taxpayer
Someone whose taxable income falls in the basic-rate band (£12,571 to £50,270 in 2026/27 for most people, rest of UK).
Battery degradation
The gradual loss of a battery's usable capacity as it ages and is cycled.
Benefit in kind (BIK)
A taxable perk from your employer, such as a company car. Tax is based on a percentage of the car's list price – 4% for zero-emission cars in 2026/27.
Bond
A loan to a government or company that pays interest and returns your money at a set date.
Buy now, pay later (BNPL)
Splitting a purchase into instalments. Interest-free "deferred payment credit" from a lender that is a different business from the seller has been regulated by the FCA since 15 July 2026, and Section 75 now applies to those agreements.
Capital Gains Tax (CGT)
Tax on the profit when you sell certain assets for more than you paid. The annual exempt amount is £3,000 in 2026/27. Gilts are exempt.
Cash ISA
An ISA holding cash savings. Interest is tax-free. You can put your whole £20,000 ISA allowance into cash; Budget 2026 scrapped a planned cut to this from April 2027.
Chargeback
A voluntary card-scheme process for asking your card provider to reverse a payment. It covers debit and credit cards, with time limits.
Clean price
A bond's quoted price, excluding accrued interest.
Collar
A minimum rate below which a tracker mortgage won't fall.
Compound interest
Interest earned on previous interest as well as on your original money.
Confirmation of Payee
An account name-checking service shown before some bank transfers, designed to help make sure you're paying the right person or business. Pay attention to any warning.
Cooling-off period
A short window to cancel after buying. For example, you can usually cancel an online purchase within 14 days of receiving it, and MoneyHelper says you have 30 days to cancel life or income protection insurance for a full refund.
Coupon
The fixed interest a bond pays, as a percentage of its nominal (face) value.
Credit reference agency (CRA)
A company holding credit records. The three main UK CRAs are Equifax, Experian and TransUnion.
Current Account Switch Service (CASS)
The free service that moves your current account, payments and Direct Debits to a new bank in 7 working days, with a guarantee.
Defined benefit pension
A pension paying an income based on salary and years of service (a 'final salary' or 'career average' scheme).
Defined contribution pension
A pension where the pot depends on contributions and investment growth. SIPPs and most workplace pensions are this type.
Direct Debit Guarantee
Protection for every Direct Debit: if the organisation or your bank makes an error, you're entitled to a full and immediate refund from your bank or building society.
Dirty price
A bond's price including accrued interest – what you actually pay.
Diversification
Spreading your money across different investments to lower the risk that one going wrong hurts you badly. It reduces risk but doesn't remove it.
Dividend
A payment a company makes to its shareholders out of profits.
Dividend Allowance
Dividend income you can receive tax-free each year – £500 in 2026/27.
Drawdown
Taking income directly from your invested pension pot while the rest stays invested.
Early repayment charge (ERC)
A fee for paying off or leaving a mortgage deal early.
Easy access account
A savings account that lets you withdraw money whenever you like.
Economy 7
An electricity meter and tariff with a cheaper rate for seven hours overnight.
Emergency fund
Money kept in an instant-access account for the unexpected. MoneyHelper's rule of thumb is three to six months' essential outgoings.
Equity
Your share of a property's value that you own outright; also another word for shares.
ETF
Exchange-traded fund – a fund that trades on a stock exchange like a share.
eVED
Electric Vehicle Excise Duty – a proposed pay-per-mile charge on electric (3p) and plug-in hybrid (1.5p) cars from April 2028. Draft legislation, not yet law.
Excess
The amount you pay yourself when you make an insurance claim. A voluntary excess can lower your premium but means you get less back when you claim.
Expensive car supplement
An extra £440 a year of vehicle tax in years 2 to 6 for cars with a list price over £40,000 (over £50,000 for zero-emission cars from April 2026).
FCA
Financial Conduct Authority – the UK regulator for financial firms and advice.
Financial association
A link on your credit files with someone you share credit with, such as a joint account. It stays until you ask for it to be removed.
First Time Buyer ISA
A new ISA announced at Budget 2026, planned for April 2029 to replace the Lifetime ISA for new savers: up to £4,000 a year with a 25% government bonus on the final balance, for a first home costing up to £450,000. Not law yet.
Fixed-rate bond
A savings account paying a fixed rate for a set term, usually with no access until it ends.
Fixed-rate mortgage
A mortgage whose interest rate stays the same for a set period.
Flexible ISA
An ISA that lets you withdraw money and pay it back in the same tax year without using more allowance.
Foreign transaction fee
A charge, often a percentage, added when you spend in a foreign currency.
FSCS
Financial Services Compensation Scheme – protects eligible deposits up to £120,000 per person, per authorised firm (from 1 December 2025). For investments, if a regulated firm fails it may pay up to £85,000 per person, per firm.
FTTC (fibre to the cabinet)
Broadband with fibre to the street cabinet and copper to your home. Speed falls with distance from the cabinet.
FTTP (full fibre)
Broadband with fibre all the way into your home – the fastest widely available connection.
Gilt
A bond issued by the UK government.
Gross
Before tax or charges are taken off.
Gross redemption yield (GRY)
The annual return on a bond held to maturity, including coupons and any gain or loss to its £100 redemption value, before tax.
Hard search
A credit check made when you apply for credit. Other lenders can see it.
Higher-rate taxpayer
Someone with taxable income between £50,271 and £125,140 (2026/27, rest of UK).
Household Energy Payment
A £100 payment announced at Budget 2026 for households in council tax bands A to D in England, from January 2027.
Index
A measure of a market's performance, such as the FTSE 100.
Index fund
A fund that aims to track an index rather than beat it. Also called a tracker fund.
Inflation
The rate at which prices rise over time, usually measured by CPI in the UK.
Interest-only mortgage
You pay only the interest each month, so the full loan must be repaid at the end.
ISA
Individual Savings Account – a tax-free wrapper for savings or investments. Allowance £20,000 in 2026/27.
Junior ISA
A tax-free account for under-18s; £9,000 a year in 2026/27.
Junior SIPP
A SIPP opened for a child by a parent or guardian. Up to £2,880 a year can go in, topped up to £3,600 by tax relief.
kWh
Kilowatt-hour – the unit energy is billed in. A 1 kW appliance running for an hour uses 1 kWh.
kWp
Kilowatt peak – a solar system's maximum output in ideal conditions.
Lifetime ISA (LISA)
An ISA for 18–39s to open, with a 25% government bonus on up to £4,000 a year, for a first home or retirement from 60. From 29 October 2026 the withdrawal charge is 20% (not 25%) if you buy a first home costing over £450,000. It's due to be replaced for new savers by the First Time Buyer ISA from April 2029.
Loan shark
An illegal moneylender, often charging very high interest. Report one anonymously: Stop Loan Sharks (England) 0300 555 2222; Wales 0300 123 3311; Scotland 0800 074 0878; Northern Ireland Consumerline 0300 123 6262.
Loan-to-value (LTV)
The size of a mortgage as a percentage of the property's value.
Lump Sum Allowance
The cap on tax-free cash you can take from pensions – £268,275 for most people.
Maturity
The date a bond or fixed account ends and your money is repaid.
Mbps
Megabits per second – the unit broadband speed is measured in.
Minimum payment
The smallest amount you must pay each month to keep a credit card account up to date. Paying only this makes debt last much longer.
Money mule
Someone who lets criminals move money through their bank account, sometimes for a payment. It's a crime and can mean up to 14 years in prison.
Money Purchase Annual Allowance
A reduced pension annual allowance (£10,000) after you've flexibly accessed a defined contribution pension.
Money transfer
Moving money from a credit card into a bank account, usually for a fee.
MVNO
Mobile virtual network operator – a mobile provider that uses another company's network, such as giffgaff or Tesco Mobile.
National Insurance
A tax on earnings and self-employed profits that also builds your entitlement to the State Pension and some benefits. For most employees in 2026/27 it's 8% of pay between £1,048 and £4,189 a month, then 2% above.
National Living Wage
The legal minimum hourly pay for workers aged 21 and over: £12.71 from 1 April 2026, rising to £13.21 from 1 April 2027 (announced at Budget 2026).
Net
After tax or charges have been taken off.
Nominal value
A bond's face value – usually £100 for gilts – which is repaid at maturity.
Normal minimum pension age
The earliest age most people can take pension benefits: 55, rising to 57 on 6 April 2028.
Notice account
A savings account where you must give notice before withdrawing.
OCF
Ongoing charges figure – a fund's yearly running costs as a percentage.
One Touch Switch
The broadband and landline switching process since September 2024: you only contact your new provider.
Overpayment
Paying more than your required mortgage payment to clear the loan sooner.
P45 / P60
A P45 is the form you get when you leave a job. A P60 is your end-of-year summary of pay and tax from an employer, due by 31 May.
PAC code
Porting Authorisation Code – text PAC to 65075 to switch mobile network and keep your number.
Packaged bank account
A current account with a monthly fee that includes extras such as travel or phone insurance.
Payday loan (high-cost short-term credit)
A small, short-term loan with a high cost. FCA rules cap interest and fees at 0.8% a day of the amount borrowed, default charges at £15, and total charges at 100% of the amount borrowed.
PAYE
Pay As You Earn: the system your employer uses to take Income Tax and National Insurance from your pay before you receive it.
Payslip
The statement your employer must give you on or before payday. It shows gross pay, deductions that vary (such as tax and National Insurance) and net pay.
Pension Tax Allowance
Announced for 6 April 2027 to 5 April 2030: people over State Pension age with total income up to £13,037 pay no income tax, tapering away up to £13,319. Being legislated in Finance Bill 2026-27, so not law yet.
Pension Tracing Service
A free GOV.UK service for finding contact details of a lost workplace or personal pension. It needs an employer or provider name and won't tell you whether you have a pension or its value.
Persistent debt
FCA term for when, over 18 months, you've paid more in interest, fees and charges than you've repaid of the borrowing on a card.
Personal Allowance
Income you can receive tax-free each year – £12,570 in 2026/27, reduced above £100,000 of income.
Personal Savings Allowance (PSA)
Savings interest you can earn tax-free: £1,000 basic rate, £500 higher rate, £0 additional rate (2026/27).
Phishing
A scam message, email, text or call pretending to be from a trusted organisation to steal your details or money. Forward scam texts to 7726 and scam emails to report@phishing.gov.uk.
Plan 2 student loan
A student loan for English students who started between 1 September 2012 and 31 July 2023 (and Welsh students from 1 September 2012). You repay 9% of income over £29,385, and any balance is written off after 30 years.
Platform
A website or app for buying and holding investments, such as funds and shares, in ISAs, SIPPs and general accounts.
Pound-cost averaging
Investing a fixed amount regularly, so you buy more units when prices are low and fewer when they're high.
Premium
What you pay for insurance cover, monthly or yearly.
Price cap
Ofgem's limit on the unit rates and standing charges suppliers can charge on standard variable (default) tariffs.
PSTN switch-off
The closure of the UK's analogue phone network. Openreach's deadline is 31 January 2027; landlines move to digital.
Qualifying year
A tax year in which you paid or were credited with enough National Insurance to count towards your State Pension.
Refused payment fee
A charge for refusing a payment because there isn't enough money. The FCA says it should reflect the bank's actual costs.
Regular saver
An account where you pay in a set amount each month, usually for a year.
Relief at source
How personal pension tax relief is usually given: you pay 80% and the provider claims 20% basic-rate relief.
Remortgage
Switching your mortgage to a new deal, with your current lender or a new one.
Representative APR
The APR at or below which a lender expects at least 51% of people accepted through an advert to be charged. Others may get a higher rate.
Representative APR
The rate a lender expects to offer to at least 51% of the people accepted for a product after an advert. Up to 49% can be offered a higher rate, so your own APR may differ.
Roaming
Using your mobile abroad on another country's network. Charges vary by provider.
Round-trip efficiency
The share of energy put into a battery that you get back out. 90% means 10 kWh in gives 9 kWh out.
Rule of 72
A rule of thumb: divide 72 by a yearly growth rate to estimate how many years it takes money to double. At 4% it gives about 18 years. It's an estimate, not an exact figure, and ignores tax, fees and changing rates.
Salary sacrifice
Giving up some salary in exchange for a non-cash benefit, such as pension contributions or an electric car.
Section 75
The Consumer Credit Act rule that makes a card provider jointly liable for purchases over £100 and up to £30,000 paid at least partly by credit card.
Self Assessment
The way you report income that isn't taxed through your payslip, such as self-employed profits, by filing a tax return with HMRC.
SIM-only
A mobile plan for calls, texts and data without a handset.
Simple interest
Interest paid only on the original amount, not on interest already added. £1,000 at 4% simple interest earns £40 a year.
SIPP
Self-Invested Personal Pension – a personal pension where you choose the investments.
Smart Export Guarantee (SEG)
A scheme requiring larger electricity suppliers to pay small generators, such as homes with solar panels, for electricity exported to the grid.
Social tariff
A cheaper broadband or phone package for people on Universal Credit and some other benefits.
Soft search
A credit check only you can see. It doesn't affect your credit score. Used by eligibility checkers.
Split contract
A mobile contract with the handset and airtime on separate plans, so you pay only for airtime once the phone is paid off.
STAC code
Service Termination Authorisation Code – text STAC to 75075 to switch mobile network without keeping your number.
Standard variable rate (SVR)
A lender's default mortgage rate, often applied when a deal ends.
Standing charge
A fixed daily charge for being connected to the energy supply, whatever you use.
Starting rate for savings
Up to £5,000 of savings interest taxed at 0%, reduced by other income above your Personal Allowance.
State Pension
A regular payment from the government once you reach State Pension age, based on your National Insurance record. The full new State Pension is £241.30 a week in 2026/27, rising to £13,036.40 a year from April 2027.
Stocks and shares ISA
An ISA holding investments such as funds and shares. Your capital is at risk.
Switching bonus
A cash payment some banks offer when you switch your current account and meet set conditions. Offers change often.
Tax code
Letters and numbers HMRC gives your employer to work out how much tax-free pay you get. 1257L is the standard code for most people with one job: the 1257 stands for the £12,570 Personal Allowance.
Tax year
In the UK, runs from 6 April to 5 April the following year.
Temporary high balance
A large sum from a life event, such as a house sale, protected by the FSCS up to £1.4 million for six months.
Third-party insurance
The legal minimum motor insurance in the UK. It covers injury or damage you cause to others, but not damage to your own vehicle.
Time-of-use tariff
An energy tariff whose unit rate changes by time of day, often with cheap overnight hours.
Tracker mortgage
A mortgage whose rate follows Bank Rate plus a set margin.
Trading allowance
£1,000 a year of tax-free income from self-employment or casual work. It's one allowance across all your side income. Above £1,000 you usually need to tell HMRC. Budget 2026 announced a rise to £3,000 from 6 April 2027 (not law yet: Finance Bill 2026-27).
Unarranged overdraft
Going overdrawn without an agreed limit, or beyond it. The interest rate can't be higher than the arranged overdraft rate.
Unit rate
The price you pay for each kWh of electricity or gas.
Vehicle-to-grid (V2G)
Technology that lets an electric car send power from its battery back to the home or the grid.
Yield
The income from an investment as a percentage of its price.
ZEV mandate
Rules requiring carmakers to make a rising share of new car and van sales zero-emission: 80% of new cars by 2030 and 100% by 2035.

Figures in definitions are for 2026/27 and are sourced on the relevant guide pages (checked 12 October 2026).
