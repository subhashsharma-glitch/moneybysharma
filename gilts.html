Gilts, explained

UK government bonds, how they're taxed, and how a gilt held to maturity compares with a savings account after tax.

What is a gilt?

A gilt is a bond issued by the UK government through the Debt Management Office (DMO). A conventional gilt pays a fixed coupon (interest) twice a year on each £100 of nominal value and repays £100 at its maturity date. Gilts trade on the market, so their price moves – mainly with interest rates. If you sell before maturity you could get back less than you paid; if you hold to maturity you receive £100 per £100 nominal.

Why low-coupon gilts can be tax-efficient

  • Capital gains on gilts are exempt from Capital Gains Tax for individuals.
  • The coupon is taxable as savings income (it can use your Personal Savings Allowance and starting rate).

A low-coupon gilt trading below £100 delivers most of its return as a tax-free gain at maturity rather than as taxable interest. For a higher- or additional-rate taxpayer, that can mean a better after-tax return than a savings account paying a similar headline rate. The calculator below shows the effect. Inside an ISA or SIPP, the tax difference doesn't apply.

How it works: bought today and held to maturity. You pay the clean price plus accrued interest (dirty price). The gross redemption yield (GRY) uses semi-annual compounding (the market convention). The net yield taxes each coupon at your chosen rate, leaves the gain to £100 untaxed (CGT-exempt), and is shown as an annual effective rate. The equivalent savings rate is the AER a savings account would need to pay to match it after tax: net ÷ (1 − tax rate). Simplifications: coupon dates are counted back from maturity, ex-dividend periods and dealing costs are ignored, and the Accrued Income Scheme (which can apply if your gilts' total nominal value is over £5,000) isn't modelled. The 2027/28 options use the higher savings rates legislated for that year. If you pay 0% on all coupons, the "equivalent" is simply the gross figure.

Prices aren't fetched automatically: the DMO's free published reference data gives each gilt's name, coupon and maturity (refreshed when the site is rebuilt), but not live dealing prices. Enter the current price from your broker.
