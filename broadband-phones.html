Broadband & phones

Connection types, what speed you need, your rights on price rises and switching, and an A–Z of providers. A neutral list – we don't rate or recommend anyone.

Information only – not advice. MoneyBySharma has no affiliation with, and receives no payment or commission from, any broadband, phone or comparison company. Nothing here is a recommendation. Prices and availability depend on your address and change often, so always check the current terms with the provider.

Broadband connection types

  • ADSL (copper). Uses the old copper phone line all the way to your home. This is the slowest fixed option, and it's being phased out as the network moves to fibre.
  • Fibre to the cabinet (FTTC, "superfast"). Fibre runs to the street cabinet, then copper runs to your home. Speeds fall the further you are from the cabinet.
  • Full fibre (FTTP). Fibre goes all the way into your home. It's the fastest and most reliable widely available option, with upload speeds often matching download speeds.
  • Cable. Uses a separate coaxial cable network, mainly Virgin Media's, which is being upgraded to full fibre.
  • 4G/5G home broadband. A router that uses the mobile network. There's no line to install, but speeds depend on the signal where you live.
  • Satellite. For places with no other option. Newer low-orbit services are much faster than older satellite broadband but need a dish and clear sky.

Speeds explained

Speeds are measured in megabits per second (Mbps). The download speed affects streaming and browsing. The upload speed affects video calls, sending files and cloud backups. Your own speed depends on your line, your Wi-Fi and how many devices are in use. A wired connection is usually faster than Wi-Fi.

Checking what's available

Availability depends on your exact address. You can check it with Ofcom's broadband and mobile checker (opens in new tab), the Openreach fibre checker (opens in new tab) or the BT Wholesale availability checker (opens in new tab). These cover networks that sell through many providers. Other full fibre networks, such as Hyperoptic, Community Fibre or YouFibre, have their own postcode checkers on their websites.

Sources (checked 12 October 2026): Ofcom – Broadband and mobile coverage checker (opens in new tab); Openreach – Fibre checker (opens in new tab); BT Wholesale – Broadband availability checker (opens in new tab).

The digital landline switchover

The old analogue phone network (the PSTN) is being switched off. Openreach says the deadline is 31 January 2027 and hasn't changed. Landlines move to digital services that usually run through your broadband router.

  • Your phone will need power. A digital landline won't work in a power cut unless you have a battery backup. Tell your provider if you rely on your landline, have poor mobile signal, or are vulnerable – they should offer a battery backup.
  • Telecare and alarms. Personal alarms, health monitors, burglar alarms, door entry systems and some card machines can be affected. Tell your provider and your alarm or telecare supplier before you switch. Providers offer extra support for telecare users, including free engineer visits and battery backups. Openreach's "Prove Telecare" engineers test alarms on the new line before completing the upgrade.
  • No broadband? Some providers offer a dedicated landline-only service for customers without broadband.
  • After 31 January 2027, Openreach says lines that haven't been upgraded may be moved to a limited, emergency-only phone service.

Sources (checked 12 October 2026): Openreach – Switching off the PSTN (31 January 2027) (opens in new tab); Openreach – PSTN closure safeguards; 31 January 2027 deadline unchanged (opens in new tab); GOV.UK – UK transition from analogue to digital landlines (opens in new tab).

Mid-contract price rises

Since 17 January 2025, Ofcom rules have banned new phone, broadband and pay-TV contracts from including price rises linked to inflation or set as a percentage. Any rise written into the contract must be shown in pounds and pence, prominently, before you sign, along with when it will happen.

If your provider raises the price when the contract doesn't say by how much, or by more than the contract allows, it must give you at least 30 days' notice. You then have 30 days to leave without paying exit fees. Contracts signed before 17 January 2025 may still have inflation-linked rises.

Sources (checked 12 October 2026): Ofcom – Price rises must be shown in pounds and pence (17 Jan 2025) (opens in new tab); Ofcom – Telecoms price rises: what are your rights? (opens in new tab).

Social tariffs

Social tariffs are cheaper broadband and phone packages for people on certain benefits. Some providers call them "essential" or "basic" broadband. If you or someone in your household claims Universal Credit, you can choose any of the tariffs. All the major providers also include Pension Credit, Employment and Support Allowance, Jobseeker's Allowance and Income Support. Ofcom keeps the full list of social tariffs (opens in new tab).

Sources (checked 12 October 2026): Ofcom – Social tariffs: cheaper broadband and phone packages (opens in new tab).

Switching broadband

  • One Touch Switch. This launched for most customers on 12 September 2024. You contact only your new provider, which arranges the switch, even between different networks. You don't pay notice-period charges beyond the switch date.
  • Exit fees. Leaving during your minimum term usually means an early termination charge. The amount is set out in your contract.
  • The right to leave. If your price rises in a way your contract didn't set out, you can leave without exit fees within 30 days of being told.
  • Automatic compensation. Providers that have signed up to Ofcom's scheme pay you as a bill credit, without you having to ask. Rates from 1 April 2026: £10.34 a day if a reported loss of service isn't fixed after two full working days, and £32.31 for a missed engineer appointment (or one cancelled with less than 24 hours' notice). There's also a daily payment if a new service starts late. Rates rise each year with inflation.
  • End-of-contract notices. Your provider must tell you 10 to 40 days before your contract ends. The notice gives the end date, what you'll pay afterwards and the provider's best deals. If you're out of contract, you must get the same information once a year.

Sources (checked 12 October 2026): Ofcom – Simpler and quicker broadband switching is here (One Touch Switch, 12 Sep 2024) (opens in new tab); Ofcom – Telecoms price rises: what are your rights? Mobile phones

  • SIM-only. Just the airtime: calls, texts and data. Contracts are usually monthly rolling or 12–24 months. It's often the cheapest option if you already have a phone.
  • Contract (handset + airtime). Includes a phone. Most UK providers now use split contracts: a separate device plan (credit, which may involve a credit check) and an airtime plan. Once the device is paid off, you pay only for the airtime.
  • Handset finance. You buy the phone on credit from the retailer or provider, often over 24–36 months. Check the total cost and whether interest is charged. You could also buy the phone outright and pair it with a SIM-only deal.
  • Networks vs MVNOs. The UK has four mobile networks with their own masts: EE, O2, Three and Vodafone (Vodafone and Three have merged). MVNOs, such as giffgaff, Tesco Mobile or SMARTY, sell services using one of those networks. Coverage depends on which network the MVNO uses.
  • Roaming in the EU. Since Brexit, free EU roaming is no longer guaranteed. Some providers include it, while others charge a daily fee or set fair-use limits. Since 1 October 2024, Ofcom rules require providers to send you a free alert when you start roaming, telling you about charges and limits. Check before you travel.
  • Unlocked phones. Since December 2021, UK mobile providers have been banned from selling locked handsets, so a phone bought from them should work on other networks.
  • Switching with a text. Text PAC to 65075 to keep your number, or STAC to 75075 if you don't want to keep it. The code arrives within a minute and is valid for 30 days. Give it to your new provider, which must complete the switch within one working day. You don't pay notice-period charges after the switch date.
