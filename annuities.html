Annuities: income estimate and providers

What an annuity is, a rough income estimate from published market rates, and the main UK providers. Information only, not advice.

Information only, not financial advice. MoneyBySharma has no affiliation with any provider listed and receives no payment or commission.

An annuity swaps some or all of your pension pot for a guaranteed income, usually for the rest of your life. Once bought it normally can't be changed or cashed in, so it's worth understanding the options first. Pension Wise (opens in new tab), from MoneyHelper, offers a free, impartial, government-backed appointment explaining your options if you're 50 or over with a defined contribution pension.

What the 25% tax-free cash does

From age 55 (57 from 6 April 2028) you can usually take up to 25% of your pension as a tax-free lump sum, up to a lifetime limit of £268,275 (the Lump Sum Allowance). If you take it before buying an annuity, only the remaining 75% buys income, so your annuity is smaller. Annuity income itself is taxable like a salary. You can't add the cash back later to boost the annuity, so think about what you'd use it for.

Enhanced annuities: health and lifestyle

If you have a health condition (such as diabetes, high blood pressure or heart problems) or lifestyle factors (such as smoking or being overweight), providers may expect to pay you for a shorter time and offer a higher income. This is called an enhanced annuity. Even common conditions can count, so it's worth answering the health questions fully when you get quotes.

Published example: for a 65-year-old buying a single life, level annuity with no guarantee, HL's table shows £8,211 a year from £100,000 at standard rates and £8,755 for someone who has smoked 10 cigarettes a day for the last 20 years and drinks 15 units of alcohol a week (quotes dated 15 Oct 2026). Our calculator shows standard rates only.

Drawdown versus annuity

AnnuityDrawdown
IncomeGuaranteed, set when you buyYou choose how much to take; it isn't guaranteed
Investment riskNone for youPot stays invested and can fall as well as rise
Running outCan't run out; pays for lifeCan run out if you take too much or investments fall
FlexibilityCan't usually change once boughtChange or stop withdrawals at any time

You don't have to choose one: some people use part of their pot for an annuity to cover essential bills and keep the rest in drawdown, or buy an annuity later, when rates for their age are higher.

Comparing quotes

You don't have to buy from your current pension provider. MoneyHelper's annuity comparison tool (opens in new tab) compares quotes across the market and is impartial and backed by government. A regulated financial adviser can recommend a product, usually for a fee.

Pension scam warning

Be wary of cold calls, texts or emails about your pension, offers of a "free pension review", promises of high returns or early access before 55, and pressure to decide quickly. Check any firm on the FCA Register before you act. See the FCA's ScamSmart (opens in new tab) guidance and its pension scams page (opens in new tab).
