Pensions, explained

The State Pension, workplace pensions and SIPPs for 2026/27 – and why the age you start can matter more than the amount. With investments your capital is at risk; past performance is not a guide to future returns.

The State Pension

  • Full new State Pension 2026/27: £241.30 a week (about £12,548 a year). This is for people who reached State Pension age on or after 6 April 2016.
  • From April 2027: the full new State Pension rises by 3.9% under the triple lock to £13,036.40 a year, £488 a year more (announced at Budget 2026).
  • You usually need 35 qualifying years of National Insurance (paid or credited) for the full amount, and at least 10 qualifying years to get anything. With 10 to 34 years you get a proportion: for example, 20 years gives 20/35 of the full rate.
  • You can check your forecast and any gaps on GOV.UK, and see your State Pension age (opens in new tab). It's currently 66 and rising to 67 between 2026 and 2028.
Budget 2026 (28 October 2026):
  • Pension Tax Allowance, 6 April 2027 to 5 April 2030: people over State Pension age whose total income is up to £13,037 won't pay income tax. The allowance tapers away for incomes between £13,037 and £13,319. It's being legislated in Finance Bill 2026-27, so it isn't law yet.
  • Triple lock change from April 2030: the full triple lock stays until April 2029. From April 2030 the State Pension is due to rise by the highest of 2.5%, inflation, or the amount needed to keep its value relative to earnings as it stands in April 2029. This is a government plan.

Workplace pensions and auto-enrolment

  • Employers must automatically enrol workers aged 22 to State Pension age who earn over £10,000 a year (the earnings trigger, unchanged for 2026/27).
  • The legal minimum is 8% of qualifying earnings, with at least 3% from your employer. Your own contribution includes tax relief.
  • Qualifying earnings for 2026/27 are those between £6,240 and £50,270 a year. Many employers pay more than the minimum or use your full salary.
  • If you opt out, you usually lose your employer's contribution.

A month into your workplace pension: a worked example

Illustration only. Take a £30,000 salary paid monthly, in a scheme paying the legal minimum on qualifying earnings (£6,240 to £50,270) with tax relief at source. Qualifying earnings are £30,000 − £6,240 = £23,760 a year.

Find lost pensions and check your State Pension

  • Pension Tracing Service (free, GOV.UK). If you've lost track of a workplace or personal pension, use the online tracing service (opens in new tab) to find the scheme's contact details. You need the name of an employer or a pension provider. It won't tell you whether you have a pension or what it is worth – you then contact the scheme. You can also ask by phone on 0800 731 0175 (Monday to Friday, 10am to 3pm) or by post.
  • State Pension forecast. Check how much State Pension you could get, when you can get it and whether you can increase it, on GOV.UK or in the HMRC app. You sign in with a GOV.UK account.
  • Beware of scams. The tracing service is free. Cold calls or "free pension reviews", and anyone offering to release money before the minimum pension age, are classic warning signs – see our pension scams section.

Sources (checked 17 October 2026): GOV.UK – Find pension contact details (Pension Tracing Service) (opens in new tab); GOV.UK – Check your State Pension forecast (opens in new tab); FCA – Pension scams (opens in new tab).

SIPPs and tax relief

A SIPP is a personal pension where you choose the investments. The rules on tax relief, the £60,000 annual allowance and access age (55, rising to 57 on 6 April 2028) are covered in our Investments guide. In short, a basic-rate taxpayer paying in £80 gets £100 in their pension. Anyone, including children and non-earners, can pay up to £2,880 a year (net) and receive tax relief, making £3,600 gross.

Junior SIPP example

A parent pays £2,880 a year into a Junior SIPP from birth until the child's 18th birthday. Tax relief tops it up to £3,600, so £64,800 goes in, of which £51,840 is from the parent. With no further contributions and the same assumptions, the pot at 67 would be about £840,774, or about £223,084 in today's money. The money is locked away until the child reaches minimum pension age (57 from 2028; it may rise further).

Time in the market vs timing the market

Trying to sell before falls and buy back before rises ("timing the market") is very hard to do consistently. Some of the biggest daily gains have come soon after the biggest falls, so investors who sell in a panic often miss the recovery.

J.P. Morgan Asset Management's Guide to Retirement 2026 illustrates this with the US S&P 500 Total Return index. $10,000 invested from 2 January 2006 to 31 December 2025 grew to $80,619 (11.0% a year) if fully invested, but only $35,866 (6.6% a year) if you'd missed the 10 best days and $21,177 (3.8% a year) if you'd missed the 20 best days. It also notes that six of the 10 best days came within two weeks of the 10 worst days. These are US market figures in dollars, gross of fees, and past performance is not a guide to future returns.

Pound-cost averaging

Paying a fixed amount in every month, as most pension savers do, means you buy more fund units when prices are low and fewer when they're high. It doesn't guarantee a profit or protect against loss, but it smooths your average purchase price and removes the temptation to guess the "right moment".

Common questions

How much is the full new State Pension?

For 2026/27 it is £241.30 a week (about £12,548 a year). From April 2027 it rises by 3.9% under the triple lock to £13,036.40 a year, as announced at Budget 2026.

How many years of National Insurance do I need for the State Pension?

You usually need 35 qualifying years for the full new State Pension and at least 10 qualifying years to get anything. With 10 to 34 years you get a proportion.

What is the minimum workplace pension contribution?

The legal minimum is 8% of qualifying earnings, with at least 3% from your employer. Qualifying earnings for 2026/27 are those between £6,240 and £50,270 a year.

When can I take money from a private pension?

The access age for personal pensions such as SIPPs is 55, rising to 57 on 6 April 2028. See SIPPs and tax relief.

How do I find a lost pension?

Use the GOV.UK Pension Tracing Service to find a scheme's contact details. You can also call 0800 731 0175 (Monday to Friday, 10am to 3pm). See finding lost pensions.
