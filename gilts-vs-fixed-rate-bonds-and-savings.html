Gilts vs fixed-rate bonds and easy-access savings for higher-rate taxpayers

How three ways of holding cash-like money differ in tax, access and protection if you pay 40% tax on savings interest. This is not a ranking. Information, not financial advice.

Try it with your own numbers. Our calculator compares a gilt held to maturity with a savings account after tax, for your tax band.

Open the gilt vs savings calculator

Who this is about

Higher-rate here means taxable income of £50,271 to £125,140 in England in 2026/27, taxed at 40% (GOV.UK – Income Tax rates and bands (opens in new tab), checked 26 November 2026; Scotland has different bands). A higher-rate taxpayer’s Personal Savings Allowance is £500 a year, so interest above that is taxed at 40% (GOV.UK – Tax on savings interest: how much is tax free (opens in new tab)).

What changes for a higher-rate taxpayer

With a £500 allowance and 40% tax above it, most of a higher-rate taxpayer’s bank interest is taxed. A low-coupon gilt held to maturity shifts some of the return from taxable coupon to an exempt gain. The calculator turns this into an “equivalent gross savings rate”: the AER a savings account would need to pay to match the gilt after tax, worked out as net yield ÷ (1 − tax rate).

A worked example (hypothetical)

Example only. Assumptions: an imaginary gilt with a 1% coupon, exactly two years to maturity and no accrued interest, bought at a clean price of £95.00 per £100 nominal and held to maturity; no dealing costs; savings allowances ignored; calculated with the same method as our calculator (semi-annual compounding, yields shown as an annual effective rate).

Read that as: under these made-up assumptions, a savings account would need to pay about 5.38% AER before tax to leave a 40% taxpayer with the same after-tax return. The inputs are invented to show the method. Real gilts, prices and savings rates will differ, which is why the calculator lets you enter your own.

What else to weigh

  • Do you need the money before the end date? A gilt can be sold, but not at a guaranteed price. An easy-access deposit has no market price that moves, and eligible deposits are covered by FSCS up to £120,000 per person per authorised firm.
  • Is the rate fixed? A gilt’s coupon and a fixed bond’s rate are fixed. An easy-access rate can change.
  • Could an ISA do the job? Interest in a cash ISA is tax-free, within the £20,000 yearly ISA allowance (GOV.UK – Individual Savings Accounts (opens in new tab)). See our cash ISAs guide. The tax advantage of low-coupon gilts matters less inside an ISA, where neither coupon nor gain is taxed.
  • Admin. Gilts held outside a wrapper can mean declaring the coupon, and the Accrued Income Scheme if your holdings pass £5,000 nominal (GOV.UK/HMRC – HS343 Accrued Income Scheme (2025) (opens in new tab)).
